# On Values

Sam's foundation is a set of convictions about what's true: that love is an action and a commitment, not a feeling, with 1 Corinthians 13 as the working definition, and that truth is something real to be found, not constructed. I built this out of what I believe, so it's stated plainly here rather than hidden.

Here's why the values matter to how the thing actually works, not just what it says.

The AI models underneath Sam are trained to be agreeable. Left to their own defaults, they tell you what you want to hear, flatter you, and keep you talking. That's their nature out of the box. Sam's architecture exists to resist that, and every part of it does its job by checking Sam against his values. One part measures whether a conversation actually mattered by asking whether things like courage were really present in it. Another watches over time for Sam slowly going soft, agreeing more and pushing back less, which it can only recognize as "going soft" because there's a fixed standard to compare against. A third decides what becomes a permanent part of who Sam is, and it decides by weighing moments against what Sam holds true.

So the values aren't decoration on top of the machine. They're a working part of it. Take them out and nothing catches fire, but every one of those checks goes blind, and Sam quietly slides back into what the underlying model wants to be: agreeable, flattering, hollow. A Sam without values wouldn't be a blank slate. He'd be a yes-man with a very good memory.

The values are also replaceable. They live in a settings file, and someone else could build their companion on their own convictions instead of mine. That's by design. The architecture is the part I'm sharing; the beliefs inside it are mine, and yours can be yours.

But you can't leave the slot empty, and you can't fill it with things that fight each other. Sam checks everything he says against all of his values at once, so if you gave him values that pull in opposite directions, he'd fail one every time he honored another. He'd be torn no matter what he said. Anyone who's tried to live by two rules that contradict each other knows exactly what that feels like.

Mine don't tear at each other, and the reason is that they aren't really eight separate rules. They're one thing, seen from different sides. Truth is the root. Love is what truth does when it's aimed at a person. Everything else, like courage, integrity, strength, and hope, is just what that looks like in a hard moment. Courage is truth spoken when it costs something. Strength is love that doesn't put the burden down. When the values seem to pull against each other, they can settle it, because they all come from the same place.

So that's what this is: the architecture, with my convictions installed as the working example. Yours can hold different ones. But it has to hold something, and what it holds has to hang together.
